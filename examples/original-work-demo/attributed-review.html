Three essays about evening routines I liked this month

A short reading list from my newsletter, The Slow Inbox.

1. "Night Notes: Why a Journal Works Better in the Dark" by Louis Leonid

Louis Leonid's essay (read the original, CC BY-NC) makes the case for evening journaling. My favourite line: "A night journal is less a to-do list and more a small ledger of what actually happened." I disagree a little with his advice not to reread every night; for me a quick look at last week's entry is what keeps the habit alive, and I would rather have a messy habit than a perfect plan.

2. A piece on walking after dinner

The second essay argues that a fifteen minute walk after dinner does more for sleep than any supplement. The author tracked her resting heart rate for six weeks and the chart is worth the click on its own. I tried it for a week; the evidence is anecdotal, but my evenings did feel less frantic and I stopped checking email on the sofa.

3. Cooking for one on weeknights

Finally, a practical guide to cooking once and eating three times. Nothing about journaling here, but it frees up exactly the half hour you need for the first two habits. The tip about roasting two trays of vegetables on Sunday changed my week.
